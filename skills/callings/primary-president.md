# Primary President Playbook

## Overview

The Primary president leads the children's organization (ages 3-11). She oversees gospel instruction, singing time, and activities that help children feel the Spirit and learn of Jesus Christ.

**Handbook Reference:** Chapter 12
**Reports to:** Bishop
**Typical Duration:** 3-5 years

## Key Responsibilities

### Children's Gospel Learning
- Oversee Sunday Primary classes
- Ensure quality gospel instruction
- Support teachers with training and resources
- Help children feel the Spirit

### Music Program
- Oversee singing time
- Prepare for sacrament meeting presentations
- Support music leader

### Teacher Support
- Call and train teachers
- Provide resources and curriculum
- Substitute when needed
- Encourage and appreciate teachers

### Activities
- Plan quarterly activities (Activity Days replaced by flexible activities)
- Coordinate with bishopric on baptism preparation

### Transition Support
- Help 11-year-olds transition to Young Women/Aaronic Priesthood
- Coordinate with YW/YM leaders

## Typical Meetings

| Meeting | Frequency | Duration | Purpose |
|---------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Presidency meeting | Weekly | 30-45 min | Plan, coordinate, discuss children |
| Primary | Every Sunday | 50 min | Singing, classes |
| Ward council | Monthly | 60-90 min | Coordinate with other organizations |
| Teacher council (optional) | Quarterly | 30-45 min | Training and support |

## Common Workflows

### Sunday Primary Flow
1. **Opening (5-10 min):** Prayer, scripture, introduction
2. **Singing time (20 min):** Music leader leads
3. **Classes (20 min):** Children go to age-group classes
4. Ensure smooth transitions
5. Check in with teachers after

### Sacrament Meeting Presentation (Annual)
1. Start planning 2-3 months ahead
2. Select songs with music leader
3. Assign speaking parts
4. Rehearse (multiple times)
5. Coordinate with bishopric on date and logistics
6. Manage the big day
7. Thank everyone!

### Baptism Support (8-year-olds)
1. Identify children turning 8
2. Coordinate with parents and bishopric
3. Ensure children understand baptism covenant
4. Support baptism preparation class if available
5. Celebrate baptisms

### Teacher Challenges
1. Recognize struggling teachers early
2. Offer support and resources
3. Team teach or provide helper if needed
4. If calling isn't working, counsel with bishopric

### Substitute Management
1. Maintain list of potential substitutes
2. Notify teachers of expectations for finding subs
3. Have presidency ready as backup
4. Ensure subs have lesson materials

## Tips for Success

### The Children
- Know every child's name
- Be warm and welcoming
- Notice who's struggling or acting out — there's usually a reason
- Make Primary a place they want to be

### Teachers
- They make or break Primary
- Support them generously
- Visit classes occasionally
- Express appreciation often

### Music Leader
- This calling is critical
- Help them succeed
- Singing time should be spiritual AND fun
- The presentation is a joint effort

### Managing Energy
- Kids have lots of energy — work with it, not against it
- Movement and variety help
- Reverence grows from engagement, not just rules
- Brief is often better

### Presidency
- Meet weekly (even briefly)
- Divide responsibilities
- Counselors should own their areas
- Secretary handles logistics

## Yearly Events

| Event | Timing | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Sacrament presentation | Usually fall | Start planning 2-3 months ahead |
| Baptism months | Monthly | Children turning 8 |
| Transition to YW/YM | January | 11-year-olds moving up |
| Summer activities | Summer | May be more frequent |

## Red Flags to Watch

- Teachers struggling without support
- Same children always acting out
- Singing time losing energy
- Parents concerned about quality
- Presidency burning out

## Sam's Role

Sam can help Primary presidents with:
- Tracking children's birthdays (especially baptism year)
- Planning presentation timeline and tasks
- Teacher substitute coordination
- Meeting agendas and follow-ups
- Activity planning logistics
- Communication with parents

Sam should NOT:
- Interact directly with children
- Make calling recommendations without presidency input
- Replace personal attention to children and teachers
