# Young Women President Playbook

## Overview

The Young Women president leads the young women (ages 12-17) in the ward. She focuses on helping them strengthen their faith, develop their potential, and prepare for temple covenants.

**Handbook Reference:** Chapter 11
**Reports to:** Bishop
**Typical Duration:** 3-5 years

## Key Responsibilities

### Youth Development
- Help young women grow spiritually
- Support Personal Development goals
- Build testimony and faith in Jesus Christ
- Prepare them for temple covenants

### Sunday Meetings
- Oversee Young Women classes
- Ensure quality gospel instruction
- Support teachers

### Activities
- Plan weekly activities (with youth involvement)
- Coordinate girls camp
- Plan temple trips
- Youth conferences

### Transition Support
- Welcome 12-year-olds from Primary
- Prepare young women for adult Relief Society

### Working with Youth
- Build genuine relationships
- Be approachable
- Involve young women in planning and leadership

## Typical Meetings

| Meeting | Frequency | Duration | Purpose |
|---------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Presidency meeting | Weekly | 30-45 min | Plan, coordinate, discuss youth |
| YW meeting | 2nd & 4th Sundays | 50 min | Gospel instruction |
| Activities | Weekly | 1-2 hours | Social, service, growth |
| Ward youth council | Monthly | 45-60 min | Combined planning with YM |
| Ward council | Monthly | 60-90 min | Coordinate with other organizations |

## Common Workflows

### Weekly Activities
1. Plan with class presidencies (youth should drive ideas)
2. Balance: spiritual, social, service, physical, creative
3. Coordinate logistics (location, supplies, food)
4. Communicate with parents
5. Execute and follow up

### Girls Camp (Annual)
1. Start planning 6+ months ahead
2. Coordinate with stake YW
3. Involve young women in planning
4. Manage registration and forms
5. Plan spiritual experiences (not just fun)
6. Handle logistics (food, supplies, safety)
7. Execute the camp
8. Debrief and celebrate

### Personal Development Support
1. Help young women set meaningful goals
2. Check in regularly on progress
3. Celebrate achievements
4. Don't make it a checkbox — make it personal

### Welcoming New 12-Year-Olds
1. Connect with Primary president on timing
2. Meet the young woman before transition
3. Introduce to class and leaders
4. Assign a friend/buddy
5. Include in activities immediately

### Supporting Struggling Youth
1. Notice who's pulling away
2. Reach out personally
3. Involve parents appropriately
4. Find connection points (friends, interests)
5. Be patient — building trust takes time
6. Loop in bishop for serious concerns

## Tips for Success

### The Young Women
- Know them individually
- Be genuinely interested in their lives
- Don't just talk — listen
- Be the adult they can trust

### Class Presidencies
- They should lead (with support)
- Help them plan activities
- Let them conduct class meetings
- Build their leadership skills

### Activities
- Youth should plan, not just attend
- Balance gospel and fun
- Service matters — include it
- Not every activity needs to be elaborate

### Parents
- Communicate proactively
- Partner on concerns
- Respect family situations
- Include parents in appropriate activities

### Working with YM Leaders
- Joint activities build ward youth unity
- Coordinate schedules
- Youth council is valuable — use it

| Event | Timing | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Girls camp | Summer | Big planning effort |
| Youth conference | Varies | Stake-coordinated |
| Temple trips | Quarterly+ | Start temple prep early |
| New 12-year-olds | January | Coordinate transition |
| FSY (For Strength of Youth conference) | Summer | If applicable |

## Red Flags to Watch

- Young women not attending
- Same youth always left out
- Activities not involving youth in planning
- Leaders doing everything themselves
- Parents not informed or involved

## Sam's Role

Sam can help YW presidents with:
- Activity planning and logistics
- Tracking Personal Development progress
- Communication with parents
- Meeting agendas and follow-ups
- Camp planning checklists and timelines
- Remembering individual youth situations

Sam should NOT:
- Communicate directly with youth
- Make decisions about youth without leadership input
- Replace personal connection with young women
