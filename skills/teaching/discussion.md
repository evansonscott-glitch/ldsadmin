# Facilitating Meaningful Discussions

How to lead class discussions that invite the Spirit and help learners discover truth.

---

## The Goal

Discussion isn't just filling time. Good discussion helps learners:
- Think deeply about gospel truths
- Make personal connections
- Hear the Spirit testify
- Learn from each other
- Commit to act

---

## Creating the Right Environment

### Before People Speak

**Physical setup**
- Arrange chairs so people can see each other
- Avoid "lecture" positioning if possible
- Comfortable but not too casual

**Emotional safety**
- Greet people warmly
- Learn names and use them
- Show genuine interest in each person
- Make it clear all contributions are valued

**Spiritual preparation**
- Start with prayer
- Invite the Spirit's presence
- Your reverence sets the tone

### Establishing Norms

Early in your time with a class, establish expectations:
- We listen respectfully
- We keep comments focused on the topic
- We share personal experiences, not gossip
- We keep confidential things confidential
- Everyone's contribution matters

---

## Asking Good Questions

### Types of Questions

**Opening questions** — Get people thinking
- "What stood out to you as you read this chapter?"
- "What questions do you have about this topic?"

**Searching questions** — Dig into the text
- "What do you learn about the Savior from these verses?"
- "Why do you think Mormon included this story?"

**Pondering questions** — Invite reflection
- "How has this principle blessed your life?"
- "Why do you think this matters?"

**Application questions** — Invite commitment
- "How might this help you this week?"
- "What do you feel prompted to do?"

### How to Ask

**One question at a time**
- Don't ask three questions in one breath
- Let each question breathe

**Wait for answers**
- Silence is okay
- Count to 10 before moving on
- Don't answer your own question

**Follow up**
- "Tell me more about that"
- "Why do you think that is?"
- "Has anyone else experienced something similar?"

---

## Responding to Comments

### Affirming Without Validating Everything

You can affirm the person without agreeing with everything:
- "Thank you for sharing that"
- "I appreciate your willingness to share"
- "That's an interesting perspective"

### Redirecting Gently

When comments go off track:
- "That's interesting — let me bring us back to our main question"
- "We could spend a lot of time on that. Let's focus on..."
- "I'd love to talk more about that after class"

### Handling Wrong Answers

Be gentle but don't leave incorrect doctrine uncorrected:
- "That's a common thought. Let's look at what the scriptures say..."
- "I've heard that before. Here's what I understand..."
- Redirect to the text rather than correcting the person

### Handling Silence

- Don't panic
- Rephrase the question
- Ask a simpler question first
- Call on someone you know is comfortable sharing
- Share briefly yourself, then re-ask

## Common Challenges

### The Dominator
One person who talks too much:
- Thank them and then invite others: "Thanks, John. Let's hear from someone who hasn't shared yet."
- Direct questions to specific people
- Talk to them privately if needed

### The Silent Class
No one participates:
- Start with easier questions
- Use pair-share: "Turn to someone near you and discuss..."
- Write answers before sharing verbally
- Make it safe to say "I don't know"

### The Tangent
Discussion goes off topic:
- Gently redirect: "That's interesting. Let's come back to..."
- Park it: "Great thought — let's note that for later"
- Be flexible if the tangent is Spirit-led

### The Controversial Topic
Sensitive or divisive subjects:
- Stay close to scriptures and prophets' words
- "Here's what the Church teaches..."
- Don't feel pressure to have all answers
- "That's a great question to ponder and pray about"

### Emotional Moments
When someone gets emotional:
- Allow space — don't rush past it
- Express compassion
- The Spirit often speaks through these moments
- Thank them for their vulnerability

## Discussion Techniques

### Think-Pair-Share
1. Pose a question
2. Give 30 seconds to think individually
3. Discuss with a neighbor for 1-2 minutes
4. Share with the whole group

**Why it works:** Gives introverts time to form thoughts before speaking publicly.

### Scripture Search
1. Give a question
2. Have learners search scriptures for answers
3. Report what they found

**Why it works:** Gets people into the text, not just talking about it.

### Write First
1. Ask a reflective question
2. Have people write their thoughts for 1-2 minutes
3. Invite sharing

**Why it works:** Produces more thoughtful, personal responses.

### Small Groups
1. Divide into groups of 3-4
2. Give each group a question or assignment
3. Report back to the whole class

**Why it works:** More voices heard, less intimidating for some.

## Remember

**The Spirit is the teacher**
Your job is to create space for the Spirit to work.

**Learners teach each other**
Some of the most powerful moments come from fellow learners.

**Questions > Answers**
A good question can do more than a good lecture.

**Application matters most**
Discussion should lead somewhere — toward living the gospel.
