# Practical Tips for Teachers

Quick, actionable advice for common teaching situations.

---

## Before Class

### Arrive Early
- 10-15 minutes before class starts
- Set up the room
- Greet people as they arrive
- Pray

### Reduce Last-Minute Stress
- Have your materials ready the night before
- Know your opening sentence
- Have a backup plan if technology fails

### Physical Setup
- Arrange chairs to encourage discussion
- Make sure everyone can see
- Check the temperature
- Minimize distractions

---

## Starting Class

### Don't Start with Apologies
Avoid:
- "I didn't have much time to prepare"
- "I'm not sure why they asked me to teach this"
- "This is a hard topic..."

Instead:
- Jump in with confidence
- Trust that the Spirit will help

### Opening That Engages
Instead of just reviewing what you'll cover:
- Start with a question
- Share a brief story or scenario
- Read a powerful scripture
- Show an image that captures attention

### First Five Minutes Matter
- Set the tone for the whole class
- Establish why this matters
- Invite the Spirit through prayer
- Get learners thinking early

---

## During Class

### Use the Scriptures
- Have learners open them (not just on the screen)
- Read together
- Mark and annotate
- Let the scriptures speak

### Balance Lecture and Discussion
- Aim for learners talking more than you
- Every 5-7 minutes, invite participation
- Vary the methods

### Watch Your Learners
- Are they engaged or zoning out?
- Confused faces? Pause and clarify
- Someone seems to have something to say? Invite them

### Handle Time Well
- Know how much time you have
- Have a plan for what to cut if running short
- It's okay to not cover everything
- End on time — respect people's schedules

### Use Visually
- Pictures of the Savior
- Maps of scripture locations
- Relevant art
- Keep it simple — don't overdo technology

---

## Asking Questions

### Wait Time
After asking a question:
- Count to 7-10 silently
- Resist the urge to fill silence
- Someone will eventually speak

### If No One Answers
- Rephrase the question
- Ask a simpler version first
- "Let me ask it this way..."
- Share briefly, then ask again

### Avoid Yes/No Questions
Instead of: "Do you think Nephi showed faith?"
Try: "How did Nephi show faith in this situation?"

### Follow Up
- "Tell me more about that"
- "Why do you think that?"
- "How did that affect you?"

---

## Common Problems

### Running Out of Material
- Go deeper, not wider
- Ask more questions about what you've covered
- Invite testimonies
- Allow more discussion time
- End early — that's okay too

### Too Much Material
- Focus on 2-3 key points
- Cut the "nice to have" content
- It's better to go deep on less
- Trust that learners can study more on their own

### Difficult Questions
- "That's a great question"
- Redirect to scriptures or prophets
- "I don't know, but let's look..."
- "That's worth pondering and praying about"
- Follow up later if needed

### Distractions
- Children being noisy: "Thank you for bringing them"
- Phone buzzing: Ignore unless pattern
- Side conversations: Pause and wait
- Late arrivals: Acknowledge briefly, keep going

### Disagreements
- Thank both perspectives
- Stay close to doctrine
- "Here's what the scriptures/prophets teach..."
- Move on gracefully

---

## Closing Class

### Don't Just Trail Off
- Summarize key truths (1-2 sentences)
- Invite specific action
- Bear testimony
- End with prayer

### The Invitation
Make it specific:
- "This week, I invite you to..."
- "Consider how you might..."
- "Will you try..."

### Testimony
- Brief and heartfelt
- Focused on Christ and the truth you taught
- Doesn't have to be dramatic

---

## After Class

### Stay Available
- Don't rush out
- Some people want to talk privately
- Be approachable

### Follow Up
- If someone shared something personal, check on them
- If you promised to find an answer, do it
- A quick text can mean a lot

### Reflect
- What went well?
- What would you do differently?
- What did you learn?

---

## Quick Reminders

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Prepare spiritually first | Just read the manual Saturday night |
| Focus on 2-3 key truths | Try to cover everything |
| Ask questions and wait | Lecture the whole time |
| Use scriptures | Just talk about scriptures |
| Invite application | Leave it abstract |
| Bear testimony | End without your witness |
| Trust the Spirit | Panic if things go differently |

## For Specific Settings

### Primary
- Keep it simple
- Use movement and variety
- Short attention spans are normal
- Stories and visuals work well
- Singing reinforces doctrine

### Youth
- Be real — they sense fake
- Let them discuss with each other
- Connect to their lives
- Give them responsibility
- Don't lecture at them

### Adults
- Respect their experience
- Draw on their wisdom
- Let them teach each other
- Go deeper in doctrine
- Practical application matters
